# Short-form Growth Funnel for Independent Music

## Purpose

Use this playbook to turn short-form videos into a measurable path from first exposure to genuine music discovery and returning listeners. It is designed for Drazyx but should be adapted to the track, source footage, platform, and current audience.

**The objective is not virality by itself.** Reach is useful only when the video attracts people who may value the music, gives them a satisfying experience, makes the artist recognizable, and offers a credible next step.

A funnel is a diagnostic model, not a guarantee that every viewer moves through every stage in order. People may discover the artist through a profile, a share, search, a friend, or a streaming platform.

## 1. The funnel

| Stage | Viewer state | Video's job | Useful formats | Primary signals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Positioning | “Is this for me?” | Make the sound, mood, and artistic world recognizable to a plausible listener | Consistent musical motifs, clear profile, recurring visual language | Audience fit, returning viewers, relevant comments |
| 1. Discovery | “What made me stop?” | Earn attention immediately with a distinctive sound, image, feeling, or truthful question | Music-first excerpt, emotional scene, strong DAW moment, visual reveal | Reach, opening retention, traffic source |
| 2. Experience | “Is this worth finishing?” | Sustain interest and deliver the opening promise | Mini-story, arrangement change, production reveal, visual progression, deliberate loop | Average view duration, average percentage viewed, completion, replays where exposed |
| 3. Resonance | “Would I keep or share this?” | Create emotional, social, or practical value worth saving or passing on | Relatable context, memorable motif, specific production insight, genuine question | Shares, saves, substantive comments |
| 4. Artist intent | “Who made this?” | Connect the moment to Drazyx and make the profile/release context clear | Artist-world series, identifiable titles, recurring visual signature, profile-aligned content | Profile visits, follows, returning viewers |
| 5. Listening action | “Where can I hear the track?” | Reduce friction to a real, available full-track destination | Release context, verified title, pinned comment/profile link where supported, clear CTA | Outbound clicks, landing-page visits, attributable platform actions |
| 6. Listener retention | “Do I want more from this artist?” | Give listeners a reason to return beyond one clip | Related tracks, series, behind-the-track stories, community participation, new musical angles | Returning listeners/viewers, follows, saves, repeat engagement, catalog activity |

Choose **one primary stage and one primary outcome per post**. A post can have secondary benefits, but trying to maximize all stages at once usually weakens the creative proposition.

## 2. What each stage should look like for Drazyx

### Discovery — earn the first seconds

Start with the most distinctive usable musical moment and an immediately readable first frame. Possible entry points:

- a melody, bass movement, vocal/lyric fragment, drum switch, or texture that distinguishes this track;
- a real FL Studio action that is audible in the music;
- an emotion or situation that the music genuinely expresses;
- a visual image that creates a specific question the video can answer.

The hook must be truthful and fulfilled. Avoid long logos, silent intros, generic “wait for it” prompts, fabricated production claims, or a visual effect that hides the music.

### Experience — reward continued attention

Plan a compact arc: **hook → development → payoff → resolution**. The size of the arc should fit the duration and track section. A short loop may need only one reveal; a longer piece may need multiple changes.

Use musical phrasing, arrangement changes, timbre, contrast, pauses, and visual evolution. A slower atmospheric piece can retain attention through controlled framing, changing focus, atmosphere, and narrative—not constant cuts. A fast track may support more subdivisions, but do not cut mechanically to every beat.

### Resonance — give the viewer something to keep or pass on

Make the content personally or socially meaningful: a recognizable feeling, a memorable musical detail, a useful production insight, a distinctive mood, or an invitation to interpret the song. Ask a specific question only when genuine responses would be useful. Do not manufacture engagement bait.

### Artist intent — make the identity memorable

Drazyx should be recognizable through a coherent relationship between sound, mood, visual motifs, typography, and creative perspective. Repetition can build recognition, but do not turn every post into the same template. Use consistent artist identification where it helps a new viewer understand who made the music.

### Listening action — create a credible handoff

Before asking someone to listen, verify the track title, version, release status, platform availability, artwork, and destination URL. Use one CTA suited to the post:

- discovery: usually no hard CTA; let the musical moment earn attention;
- connection: invite a specific interpretation or response;
- artist intent: invite a profile visit or follow;
- listening action: point to the verified full track or a working link.

Avoid asking for a follow, like, save, comment, share, pre-save, and stream in one post. Do not say “full track out now” unless it is live on that destination.

### Listener retention — build a relationship, not a one-off view

Develop recurring formats that reveal different dimensions of the artist: music-first excerpts, production/craft, artist-world context, release stories, and real community responses. Revisit a track with a distinct musical or narrative angle rather than reposting the same edit with cosmetic changes.

## 3. Content portfolio

Use these as flexible content pillars; assign each post a primary funnel stage.

| Pillar | Examples | Typical role |
|---|---|---|
| Music-first discovery | Strongest melody/drop/transition, emotional excerpt, memorable loop | Discovery and experience |
| Production and craft | FL Studio screen capture, sound-design reveal, before/after, arrangement detail | Experience and resonance |
| Artist world | Atmosphere, original visual narrative, references/inspirations that are genuinely relevant | Resonance and artist intent |
| Release narrative | Teaser, song context, artwork, release announcement, post-release rediscovery | Artist intent and listening action |
| Community | Listener interpretation, reply video, feedback, remix or UGC where rights permit | Resonance and retention |

A practical evergreen system should not depend on a release being imminent. A release campaign can temporarily increase release-story and listening-action posts, but the ongoing content engine should continue to introduce the sound, craft, and artist identity.

## 4. Hook and edit design checklist

For every concept, write:

1. **Listener hypothesis:** who might care, and why is that grounded in the music or evidence?
2. **First frame:** what is understandable on a small phone screen immediately?
3. **First sound:** which exact part of the actual track starts the video?
4. **Promise:** what feeling, detail, transformation, or question is being offered?
5. **Progression:** what changes visually or musically to justify staying?
6. **Payoff:** where is the promise delivered?
7. **Artist link:** how does the viewer identify Drazyx without distracting from the music?
8. **CTA:** what single next action, if any, serves the selected funnel stage?
9. **KPI:** which observable metric will indicate whether this post did its job?

Do not apply a fixed “three-second rule,” ideal duration, cut frequency, or hook formula to every post. Test openings and durations against the actual track, target viewer, platform, and post data.

## 5. Platform and algorithm discipline

TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts are separate recommendation and discovery environments. Adapt packaging, caption, title, sound use, and profile handoff to each platform. Cross-posting can save production time, but do not assume identical distribution or audience response.

When advice depends on current recommendation signals, eligibility, original-content rules, music usage, safe areas, metadata, or platform specifications:

- check current official documentation for the exact platform and date;
- distinguish official statements from creator observations and hypotheses;
- avoid claiming access to proprietary ranking weights or “algorithm secrets”;
- do not treat hashtags, posting time, daily frequency, watch time, shares, or completion as universal single-factor explanations;
- do not use third-party watermarked exports as a default cross-posting strategy.

The platform's own analytics labels and available metrics can change. Use what is exposed for the selected account and date range; do not fabricate unavailable measures.

## 6. Measurement and attribution

Record the platform, format, duration, publication date, observation window, audience context, and metric denominator. Compare like with like: same platform, similar duration, comparable audience, and comparable time since publication.

Useful calculations when the necessary data is available:

- **Average percentage viewed** = average view duration ÷ video duration × 100. It can exceed 100% when replays are included; metric definitions vary by platform.
- **Share rate** = shares ÷ reach (or views, if reach is unavailable) × 100.
- **Save rate** = saves ÷ reach (or views) × 100.
- **Profile-visit rate** = profile visits ÷ reach (or views) × 100.
- **Follow conversion from profile** = follows attributed to the post/profile ÷ profile visits × 100, only when both figures are available and definitions align.
- **Outbound click rate** = outbound link clicks ÷ profile visits or link impressions × 100; state the actual denominator.
- **Track-action rate** = attributable track saves or other verified listening actions ÷ measured landing-page visits or clicks × 100, only when the sources can be connected reliably.

Do not mix platform definitions or compare raw counts across platforms as if they were equivalent. Views are not unique people, clicks are not streams, and streams are not necessarily unique listeners.

For streaming attribution, use a verified destination and available analytics. UTM parameters, smart links, distributor dashboards, and platform-for-artists dashboards may help where supported. State attribution limits: a listener may search for the song manually, use another device, or listen later, so many conversions cannot be attributed to one post with certainty.

## 7. Diagnose the weak stage

| Observation | Likely issue to investigate | Next controlled test |
|---|---|---|
| Reach but weak opening retention | First frame/audio mismatch, unclear hook, wrong audience expectation | Change opening frame or audio excerpt; keep the rest stable |
| Good opening, middle drop | Repetition, no progression, delayed payoff, visual/music mismatch | Shorten or restructure the middle; add one meaningful reveal |
| Strong completion, few shares/saves | Watchable but not personally/socially valuable | Test a more specific emotion, insight, or identity connection |
| Engagement but few profile visits | Artist identity or reason to discover more is unclear | Improve attribution to the artist and profile continuity |
| Profile visits but few follows | Profile does not explain the artist or offer a coherent next step | Improve bio, pinned content, visual/music consistency |
| Clicks but few listening actions | Broken/mismatched destination, release unavailable, weak landing handoff, audience mismatch | Verify link and release availability; test a clearer track-specific promise |
| Views rise but returning audience does not | One-off reach without a repeatable artist relationship | Build a related series and compare returning-viewer/listener behavior |

These are diagnostic hypotheses, not proof of causation. Check the data and context before changing the edit.

## 8. Experiment design

For every post or variant, write a hypothesis in this form:

> If we change **[one major variable]** for **[listener hypothesis]**, we expect **[specific funnel signal]** to change because **[reason grounded in the creative]**.

Test one major variable at a time where practical: opening, audio excerpt, visual premise, pacing, text, or CTA. Keep the other major choices stable enough to interpret the result. Avoid calling a winner from a small or incomparable sample.

Use the artist's own historical baseline as the primary reference. Do not invent universal benchmarks or conclude a technique is successful from one post.

## 9. Final decision rule

Choose a creative direction that balances:

- musical distinctiveness and emotional truth;
- immediate comprehension and a meaningful attention arc;
- recognizable Drazyx identity;
- platform-appropriate packaging;
- a relevant next step for the intended funnel stage;
- feasible production effort and repeatability.

The best result is not necessarily the most edited or most viewed clip. It is the strongest truthful piece of content for the intended listener and objective.
