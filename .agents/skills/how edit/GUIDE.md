# Editing Guide — musical pulse accents

Use this as a focused supplement to `skills/drazyx-video/SKILL.md` and `references/video-postproduction.md`. It is not a standalone editing workflow.

## Pulse zoom, glint, and shake

A kick pulse or visual accent can make static DAW footage feel more physical, but only when it reinforces a real musical event.

1. Listen to the actual track and identify a meaningful transient or phrase event. Do not place an effect from BPM alone or assume every kick deserves an accent.
2. Check the corresponding footage. Keep the effect anchored to the real focal detail: playhead, arrangement region, plugin control, artwork element, or another visible subject.
3. Choose one accent that serves a clear purpose:
   - **Pulse zoom:** a brief scale increase and controlled return to the original framing.
   - **Glint/flash:** a restrained, localized light accent that does not wash out the interface or text.
   - **Shake:** a short, low-amplitude movement used only when the track's energy justifies it.
4. Shape the animation with a quick, controlled rise and a smoother settle. Avoid abrupt snapping, overshoot, continuous oscillation, or motion that makes small DAW text unreadable.
5. Leave visual space before and after the accent. Vary or omit it when the melody, pause, or reverb tail needs to breathe.
6. Preview at phone size and inspect the actual rendered frames. Compare the accent against a version without it; keep it only if it improves focus, impact, or musical connection.

## Quality limits

- Do not pulse on every beat by default.
- Do not stack zoom, shake, flash, glitch, and blur on the same transient.
- Do not add random effects to compensate for footage that has no clear idea.
- Do not invent DAW activity or imply that a visible action created a sound unless the recording proves it.
- Do not copy a reference clip's exact timing, composition, graphics, or distinctive expression.

## Required outcome

The viewer should notice the music or the meaningful screen detail first. The effect is successful when it strengthens that moment without becoming the subject of the edit.
